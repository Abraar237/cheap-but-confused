# Recency and pre-emption re-check, 2026-10-10

Paper: "Cheap but Confused? The accuracy cost of cache-optimized context layouts in LLM agents"

Claim under test: nobody has held an agent's context information content fixed, varied only its LAYOUT (append-only prefix-stable vs relevance-reordered vs pruned vs stable-prefix + appended recitation block), in a multi-turn tool-using agent, and measured task accuracy against provider-reported cached tokens and billed USD.

Four criteria used throughout: (a) content-fixed layout manipulation; (b) multi-turn tool-using agent; (c) provider-reported cached tokens / billed USD; (d) accuracy outcome. Coverage codes in the table: Y = yes, P = partial, N = no.

## 1. Verdict: ALIVE-BUT-CROWDED (and more crowded than on the January-September sweep)

No paper found satisfies all four criteria. The nearest miss is **2607.12161 "Token Reduction Is Not Cost Reduction"** (Weinberger and Hozez, v1 13 Jul 2026, v5 12 Aug 2026), which satisfies (b), (c) and (d) on Claude Code with provider-billed cost decomposed into cache creation and cache reads, and SWE-bench Go patch success, but its manipulation is tool-output compression (content changes), not a content-fixed layout change. It fails (a). Its headline finding (prompt-cache creation and reads dominate input-side cost, compression can raise the bill by altering trajectories, so optimization must be judged as cost per successful task including cache behavior) is the framing our paper also adopts; we must cite it as the paper that established the measurement standard and position ours as the layout-only instrument it did not run.

The second nearest is **2610.04433 "What Does a Harness Buy? Tokens, Mostly"** (Liu and Han, 3 Oct 2026): five models x three harnesses on SWE-bench Verified, pass rate plus cost per task at one price list, with the explicit finding that "the provider's price for cached input scales the bill and does not reorder it." It compares whole harnesses (system prompt, tools, context management all differ), so it fails (a), and its cost is repriced rather than provider-billed telemetry (P on c). It is a direct neighbor on the cost side and supplies a useful null: cached pricing did not change the harness cost ranking.

The third is **2601.06007 "Don't Break the Cache"** (unchanged since v2, 31 Jan 2026; still no accuracy outcome). No follow-up by Lumer et al. was found.

Nothing submitted in September or October 2026 satisfies (a). The September/October wave is almost entirely (i) compaction/folding methods that change content while trying to preserve the cached prefix (CADOC 2609.37012, ReFold 2610.07863, CliffCompaction 2609.26779, Continuous Context Management 2609.35540, ReCAP 2609.40118), and (ii) serving-side KV work (KVTether 2609.39819, HEAR 2610.06597, Request Order Matters 2610.05833, RR-Evict 2609.32278, EfficientAgent 2609.33762). None reports provider-billed USD with cached-token telemetry alongside accuracy for a content-fixed layout change.

Two of the known nine have moved: TokenPilot 2606.17016 is now **EMNLP 2026 Findings** (v2 28 Aug 2026), which raises the bar for citing it as the "named trade-off" paper; ContextPipe and the rest are unchanged. All nine ids verified by the arXiv API with matching titles (Section 4).

Why ALIVE: the four-way intersection is still open and the gap is now stated almost verbatim by the neighbors themselves (2607.12161: evaluate "cost per successful task, including cache behavior, trajectory changes, and correctness"; 2609.37012: "efficient compression depends not only on shortening prompts but also on scheduling edits to preserve cache reuse"; 2610.04433: harness cost is "set at the first call, by the preamble ... scaled by the number of steps"). Why CROWDED: at least six 2026 papers now measure accuracy and some notion of cache-aware cost in tool-using agents, and the compaction-method papers each include a "full history" control that is, in effect, our append-only arm. A reviewer will ask what the relevance-reordered and recitation arms add beyond those controls; those two arms, plus provider-billed cache telemetry, are the paper's defensible novelty.

## 5. Detailed notes on the closest new items

### 2607.12161 Token Reduction Is Not Cost Reduction (Weinberger, Hozez; v1 2026-07-13, v5 2026-08-12; no venue listed)
Summary: three token-reduction tools vs unmodified Claude Code on controlled coding tasks, measuring provider-billed cost, task success, cache traffic and agent behavior; the largest compression cut delivered tool-output tokens 38.4% but raised billed cost 6.8%; token reduction and cost reduction correlate at r = 0.15; cache creation and reads dominate input-side cost; aggressive compression reduced successful patch application on a SWE-bench Go subset.
Does: (b) Claude Code multi-turn tool use; (c) provider-billed cost with cache creation/read decomposition; (d) task success and patch application.
Does not: (a) hold content fixed or vary order/position; its arms are compression levels. No relevance-reordered arm, no recitation arm, no prefix-stability arm, single provider, no cached-token-vs-accuracy frontier.
Implication: cite as the paper that showed cache traffic dominates agent cost and that compression is not free; our paper is the content-fixed layout counterpart.

### 2610.04433 What Does a Harness Buy? Tokens, Mostly (Liu, Han; 2026-10-03; 22 pages)
Summary: five models x three harnesses (Claude Code, mini-SWE-agent, OpenCode) on SWE-bench Verified with reruns to calibrate noise; harness effects on pass rate are within rerun noise except an OpenCode loss; cost per task differs up to 3x, set by the per-step preamble (system prompt + tool schemas) times step count; cached-input price scales but does not reorder the bill.
Does: (b), (d); (c) partially (one price list, cached-input pricing modeled, not provider telemetry).
Does not: (a); harnesses differ in content, tools and context management simultaneously. Supplies a power analysis (45 tasks resolve a 13-point gap only half the time; 447 tasks resolve 5 points) that our sample-size plan should cite.

### 2609.37012 CADOC (Yao, Zhou, Xu; 2026-09-29)
Summary: replaces structured objects with retrievable Cards and schedules the replacements in batches by an economic-order-quantity rule trading waiting cost against shared cache-reconstruction cost; ~40% lower input cost at task performance close to full context.
Does: cache-aware timing of context edits with a task-performance check.
Does not: content-fixed ordering; provider-billed USD or cached-token telemetry stated; standard agent benchmark named in the abstract. Closest in spirit to our "pruned" arm, as a method rather than a measurement.

### 2610.07863 ReFold (Su, Tian, Zhang, Kundu; 2026-10-06; 27 pages)
Summary: training-free rendering layer that stubs repeated content and folds finished turns, chunked so the cached prefix is rewritten every few steps; up to 2.5x fewer tokens, half the KV memory per session, no task-success degradation on five benchmarks and two frontier LLMs; cost cut up to 3.4x under serving.
Does not: content-fixed layout; provider-billed USD; it explicitly names the "append-only interaction history" as the baseline, so it is the strongest statement yet that append-only is the default practitioners pay for.

### 2609.04748 Same Request, Different Answer (Patodiya; 2026-09-04; submitted to IEEE Access)
Summary: in an 80-episode multi-turn agentic tool-use workload on open-source engines, enabling prefix caching changed the agent trajectory on 36.2% of episodes at 16-bit and 75.0% at 4-bit, with no shift in aggregate accuracy; cached serving is deterministic given cache state but that state is absent from the request.
Implication for us: a confound for any self-hosted (vLLM) arm: differences between layouts can be partly cache-state nondeterminism. Fix seeds, run paired episodes, and report trajectory divergence as well as accuracy; cite this.

### 2609.35540 Continuous Context Management (Hoy et al.; 2026-09-28)
Summary: compaction at every turn (task + memory + newest observation) on TerminalBench-2 with Claude Sonnet 4.6, Opus 4.6, GLM-5, Kimi K3 lowers success for most models while cutting input usage; RL with privileged full-history distillation recovers some of it on WebShop/Endless Terminals.
Implication: a direct accuracy cost of the extreme pruned layout on a tool-using agent with frontier models, but no cache accounting or USD.

### 2607.10569 execute_code regime x agent-design ablation (Yang, Yu, Desell; 2026-07-12; KDD 2026 SE 3.0 workshop)
Summary: three tool-surface arms on Claude Code and Codex CLI, holding model, harness and prompts fixed; pass rates tied, cost differences live in "cache-adjusted cost". Varies tooling not layout, but it is a second instance of the "accuracy flat, cache-adjusted cost moves" pattern on real harnesses.

### 2607.19214 Keeping the Cache Warm Pays (Khailo; 2026-07-21; 5 pages)
Summary: client-side keepalive across Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, DeepSeek keeps the prefix warm through tool pauses, cutting post-pause cost up to 12.5x; derives optimal ping interval and break-even. Real provider economics, no accuracy, no layout. Relevant to our cost model: TTL expiry during tool execution is a nuisance variable we must control (keep inter-call gaps under the shortest provider TTL or log them).

### 2609.13692 Prefix Sharing Is a Sorting Problem (He; 2026-09-12) and 2606.19667 CacheWeaver
Both treat chunk ordering with content fixed as a cache optimization and both ignore accuracy in agents (CacheWeaver checks RAG quality on vLLM). Together with 2505.15561 they are the (a)-satisfying cluster; none has (b) or (c).

### 2607.01792 PARTREP (Widjaja; 2026-07-02)
Summary: appending a (partial) second copy of the prompt redistributes grounding across positions and improves reasoning on eight benchmarks; full repetition doubles KV cache. This is the closest published mechanism argument for our recitation arm (stable prefix + appended restatement); single-turn, no agents, no pricing. Cite as motivation for why an appended recitation block could recover accuracy.

### 2606.22953 Plans Don't Persist (Mehta; 2026-06-22)
Summary: plan signal in hidden states decays within one step after the plan; naive plan eviction cuts ALFWorld success 34.7pp; probe-gated re-surfacing does not recover it. Motivates the recitation arm and warns that re-surfacing alone may not suffice.

## 6. What this changes for the paper

1. Related-work must now lead with 2607.12161 and 2610.04433 as the two measurement papers that established cost-per-successful-task with cache behavior on real coding harnesses, then state precisely that neither varies layout with content fixed and neither has a reordered or recitation arm.
2. The pruned arm now has many method-paper neighbors (CADOC, ReFold, CliffCompaction, CCM, ReCAP, TokenPilot). Our pruned arm should be a simple, pre-registered, content-reducing baseline positioned as a reference point, not a contribution; the contribution is the order-only and recitation arms under provider-billed telemetry.
3. Add 2609.04748 to the threats section: cache-state nondeterminism in self-hosted engines can masquerade as a layout effect. Pair runs, fix seeds, report trajectory divergence.
4. Add 2607.19214 to the design: control inter-call gaps against provider TTLs so cache misses from expiry are not confused with misses from layout.
5. Cite 2610.04433's power analysis when justifying task counts (447 tasks resolve ~5 points on SWE-bench Verified).
6. TokenPilot is now an EMNLP 2026 Findings paper; cite the venue.
7. The (a)-satisfying cluster (2505.15561, 2606.19667, 2609.13692) remains RAG/serving only; the null from 2505.15561 is still the hypothesis the order-only arm must test in the agent setting.

Submission timing: the intersection has been approached from the cost side (July, October) and the method side (September, October) within the last 90 days. A controlled measurement paper remains unclaimed, but the window is narrowing; a 2027 Q1 submission is the realistic target.
